\begin{abstract}
Covariate-aware time-series foundation models can improve forecasts, but accurate
predictions do not establish that known-future inputs affect forecasts at the correct
horizons. We introduce CovIntervene-SHAPE, a paired intervention audit that holds the
target history and innovations fixed while comparing predicted and structural response
paths at temporal widths 1, 2, 4, and 8. We evaluate frozen Chronos-2 and TimesFM-3
checkpoints on four synthetic mechanisms and 192 series per backbone--mechanism cell.
Three of eight cells satisfied the predeclared complete rule: direction agreement was
0.973--0.982 and gain-ratio intervals remained inside the coarse envelope, yet
fine-resolution shape distance was 0.185--0.198 and the hidden-distortion gap was
0.040--0.066. In these cells, covariate-aware scaled quantile loss was 51.4--52.3\%
lower than for the matched target-only call, so the distortion was not merely gross
forecast failure. Post-primary fitted references nearly recovered the additive linear
mechanisms but not path-dependent hysteresis or covariate synergy; a frozen nonlinear
feature expansion only partially recovered the interaction. For the tested checkpoints
and mechanisms, temporal aggregation can therefore conceal horizon-wise response
misplacement despite good forecast skill and credible coarse response behavior.
\end{abstract}

\section{Results}
\input{results/p1_shape_primary_table}
Three of eight cells passed the complete frozen rule: Chronos-2 on biphasic rebound
and dispersed delayed pulse, and TimesFM-3 on biphasic rebound
(Table~\ref{tab:p1-shape-primary}). Across these cells, DSA was 0.973--0.982 and the
complete RGR intervals remained inside the frozen $[0.65,1.35]$ envelope. Nevertheless,
$D_1$ was 0.185--0.198 and the hidden-distortion gap was 0.040--0.066, with all lower
95\% bounds above the corresponding frozen thresholds. Relative SQL was
$-0.514$ to $-0.523$, so covariate-aware forecasts also improved on their matched
target-only calls in these cells.

\input{results/p1_reference_table}
The post-primary references provide a mechanism-level check rather than another
continuation test. Ridge-ARX nearly recovered the two additive temporal responses
($D_1=0.038$ and $0.057$), but its hysteresis response remained distorted
($D_1=0.243$) and its synergy interaction distance was 1.000. The frozen nonlinear
feature expansion reduced the synergy interaction distance to 0.300 and changed its
DSA/RGR from 0.784/0.724 to 0.871/1.042. It was not uniformly stronger: its $D_1$ and
SQL were both higher than Ridge-ARX on the two additive mechanisms. These fitted
references therefore show that low response-shape error is attainable under the same
context length while also exposing mechanism-specific limitations; they do not enter
or modify the primary decision.
