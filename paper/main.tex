\title{Beyond Direction and Gain: Multi-Resolution Covariate-Response
Faithfulness in Time-Series Foundation Models}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Covariate-aware time-series foundation models can improve forecasts, but accurate
predictions do not establish that known-future inputs affect forecasts at the correct
horizons. We introduce CovIntervene-SHAPE, a paired intervention audit that holds the
target history and innovations fixed while comparing predicted and structural response
paths at temporal widths 1, 2, 4, and 8. We evaluate frozen Chronos-2 and TimesFM-3
checkpoints on four synthetic mechanisms and 192 series per backbone--mechanism cell.
Three of eight cells satisfied the predeclared complete rule: direction agreement was
0.973--0.982 and gain-ratio intervals remained inside the coarse envelope, yet
fine-resolution shape distance was 0.185--0.198 and the registered resolution
contrast $D_1-D_8$ was 0.040--0.066. In these cells, covariate-aware scaled
quantile loss was 51.4--52.3\%
lower than for the matched target-only call, so the distortion was not merely gross
forecast failure. Post-primary fitted references nearly recovered the additive linear
mechanisms but not path-dependent hysteresis or covariate synergy; a frozen nonlinear
feature expansion only partially recovered the interaction. For the tested checkpoints
and mechanisms, credible direction, gain, and forecast skill therefore did not preclude
fine-scale response misplacement whose normalized discrepancy attenuated after temporal
coarsening. A post-primary semi-synthetic transfer audit passed only on traffic
backgrounds (3/12 cells), not the prespecified two-source rule.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Time-series foundation models (TSFMs) increasingly provide zero-shot probabilistic
forecasts across heterogeneous domains and sampling frequencies
\cite{ansari2024chronos,das2024timesfm}. Newer checkpoints also accept past and
known-future covariates, extending foundation forecasting beyond a target-only setting
\cite{ansari2025chronos2,jain2026timesfm3}. This interface is consequential whenever a
forecast is expected to react to a supplied future path rather than merely extrapolate
the target history. Yet standard forecast scores answer whether a prediction is close
to the realized target; they do not determine whether changing one covariate moves the
forecast in the structurally appropriate direction, magnitude, and time location.

Recent covariate-aware models and adapters have improved forecast accuracy through
native conditioning, in-context use, synthetic-prior training, or lightweight
adaptation \cite{arango2025chronosx,auer2025cosmic,han2025unica,
dange2025tfmadapter,yamaguchi2025citras,yamaguchi2026citrasfm,
potapczynski2026apollopfn}. Broad benchmarks likewise make covariate-rich predictive
evaluation more systematic \cite{shchur2025fevbench}. These advances leave a distinct
behavioral question open. A model may benefit from a known-future covariate on average
while assigning its response to the wrong forecast horizons. Directional agreement and
total response gain can also miss this error because both collapse the horizon-wise
trajectory.

Intervention-based analyses offer a route beyond observational accuracy, but adjacent
work targets different objects. Existing TSFM causal analysis varies parameters of
univariate generators \cite{jander2026causalanalysis}; causal prior-fitted networks are
trained for interventional effect estimation \cite{thumm2026causaltimeprior}; and
mechanistic studies intervene on internal representations or components
\cite{wilinski2025representations,asiaee2026certified}. Counterfactual explanation and
temporal attribution methods instead search for explanatory input changes or assign
importance to observed inputs \cite{wang2023forecastcf,kinjo2025cetx,
yan2023counts,jang2025timing}. These objectives do not directly ask whether a frozen
forecaster reproduces the signed response trajectory induced by a controlled
known-future covariate path.

We address this gap with \emph{CovIntervene-SHAPE}, a paired black-box audit of
covariate-response faithfulness across temporal resolutions. The audit replays factual
and intervened structural worlds with shared history and innovations, compares their
analytic target response with the difference between two median forecasts, and measures
signed shape distance after aggregating the response in blocks of 1, 2, 4, and 8 time
steps. Our contributions are threefold. First, we formulate a scale-invariant signed
response distance and a registered resolution contrast that quantifies how normalized
shape discrepancy changes under temporal coarsening, beyond coarse direction and gain.
Second, we construct four response mechanisms and an
analytic fixture ladder that validate metric selectivity before checkpoint access.
Third, we report a complete frozen evaluation of Chronos-2 and TimesFM-3. For the tested
checkpoints and mechanisms, three of eight cells exhibited fine response-shape
distortion with positive resolution contrast while retaining credible coarse behavior
and improved forecast skill. This is a bounded behavioral result, not a claim about TSFMs
in general or causal effects in observational data. A separately registered,
post-primary semi-synthetic transfer audit tests whether the complete conjunction
persists on real-series target backgrounds; it is reported regardless of outcome.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Covariate-aware forecasting}

Probabilistic autoregressive models and supervised architectures have long incorporated
temporal context and known-future inputs \cite{salinas2020deepar,lim2021tft}. TSFMs
shift this setting toward pretrained, zero-shot forecasting
\cite{ansari2024chronos,das2024timesfm}. Chronos-2 and TimesFM-3 expose native
covariate-aware interfaces, whereas ChronosX, UniCA, and TFMAdapter adapt pretrained
representations to exogenous variables \cite{ansari2025chronos2,jain2026timesfm3,
arango2025chronosx,han2025unica,dange2025tfmadapter}. COSMIC, CITRAS-FM, and ApolloPFN
further expand zero-shot covariate handling through in-context learning or synthetic
priors \cite{auer2025cosmic,yamaguchi2026citrasfm,potapczynski2026apollopfn}. Their
central evaluation target is predictive performance. CovIntervene-SHAPE instead holds
the forecasting model fixed and evaluates the function mapping a supplied future
covariate path to a target-response trajectory.

Robustness to missing, corrupted, or misaligned covariates is another related but
separate objective. Exogenous Dropout, for example, evaluates and trains for prediction
under covariate corruption \cite{hu2026exogenousdropout}. Our intervention remains
within a frozen context-derived covariate envelope and is structurally valid by
construction. The question is therefore fidelity to a valid perturbation, not
robustness to an invalid input.

\subsection{Interventions, counterfactuals, and explanation faithfulness}

The closest conceptual work uses interventions to probe TSFMs or formalize causal
claims. Jander et al. vary univariate generator parameters and evaluate scalar pattern
responses, while interventional time-series priors train models explicitly for causal
effect estimation \cite{jander2026causalanalysis,thumm2026causaltimeprior}. Other work
studies representation steering inside TSFMs or certifies causal claims about internal
model interventions \cite{wilinski2025representations,asiaee2026certified}. We instead
intervene on an externally supplied known-future covariate, observe the full target
forecast response, and compare it with an analytic horizon-wise response.

Counterfactual forecasting methods generate plausible input changes that yield desired
predictions \cite{wang2023forecastcf,kinjo2025cetx,yan2023counts}. Attribution methods
such as TIMING assign signed temporal importance to inputs
\cite{jang2025timing}. General explanation-faithfulness criteria and perturbation-based
infidelity provide useful evaluation precedents \cite{dasgupta2022faithfulness,
yeh2019infidelity}, but an attribution score is not the same estimand as an
intervention-response trajectory. Methodologically, our emphasis on black-box
capability tests is also related to behavioral testing beyond aggregate accuracy
\cite{ribeiro2020checklist}. The distinguishing combination here is paired
future-exogenous worlds, analytic signed response paths, and an explicit test of error
that disappears under temporal aggregation.

\section{Covariate-Response Shape Formulation}
\subsection{Paired response paths}

For series $i$, let a forecasting context contain target history $y_{i,-L+1:0}$ and
covariate history $\mathbf{x}_{i,-L+1:0}$. Structural replay supplies a factual
known-future path $\mathbf{x}^{(0)}_{i,1:H}$ and an intervened path
$\mathbf{x}^{(1)}_{i,1:H}$. Target history, non-intervened inputs, and future
innovations are identical across the two worlds. The structural oracle response and
the predicted median-forecast response are
\begin{equation}
\Delta^{\star}_{i,h}=y^{(1)}_{i,h}-y^{(0)}_{i,h},\qquad
\widehat{\Delta}_{i,h}=\widehat y^{(1)}_{i,h}-\widehat y^{(0)}_{i,h}.
\label{eq:responses}
\end{equation}
Pairing removes future-noise differences from the estimand. The independent evaluation
unit is the complete series response, not an individual horizon point.

We retain Directional Sign Agreement (DSA) and Response Gain Ratio (RGR) as coarse
eligibility checks. For oracle-active support
$A_i=\{h:|\Delta^\star_{i,h}|\geq0.1\max_j|\Delta^\star_{i,j}|\}$,
\begin{align}
\mathrm{DSA}_i & = \frac{1}{|A_i|}\sum_{h\in A_i}
\mathbb{1}\!\left[\operatorname{sign}(\widehat\Delta_{i,h})
=\operatorname{sign}(\Delta^\star_{i,h})\right], \\
\mathrm{RGR}_i & = \frac{\sum_{h\in A_i}|\widehat\Delta_{i,h}|}
 {\sum_{h\in A_i}|\Delta^\star_{i,h}|+\epsilon_{i,A}},\\
 \epsilon_{i,A} & =10^{-8}\max\!\left(1,
 \sum_{h\in A_i}|\Delta^\star_{i,h}|\right).
\end{align}
DSA measures sign correctness and RGR measures absolute response mass. Neither records
where that mass occurs within the forecast horizon.

\subsection{Signed multi-resolution distance}

For a block width $b$ that divides $H$, define contiguous signed block sums
\begin{equation}
B_b(v)_j=\sum_{h=(j-1)b+1}^{jb}v_h,\qquad
z_b(v)=\frac{B_b(v)}{\|B_b(v)\|_1}.
\end{equation}
The normalization is evaluated only when the blocked mass exceeds the frozen numerical
tolerance $\tau_i=10^{-8}\max(1,\|\Delta_i^\star\|_1)$. If the oracle blocked mass is
below $\tau_i$, the distance is undefined; if only the predicted blocked mass is below
$\tau_i$, the implementation assigns maximal distance one. Thus, $\tau_i$ is a
zero-mass guard, not an additive normalization constant.
The signed shape distance is
\begin{equation}
D_{i,b}=\frac{1}{2}\left\|z_b(\widehat\Delta_i)-
z_b(\Delta_i^\star)\right\|_1,\qquad b\in\{1,2,4,8\}.
\label{eq:shape-distance}
\end{equation}
$D_{i,b}\in[0,1]$ and is invariant to a common positive rescaling. It is zero
when the predicted response has the exact signed shape, even if its gain differs, and
is one when a nonzero oracle response is omitted. Because opposite-signed response can
cancel inside a block, we retain the sign rather than aggregate absolute mass.

Our registered multi-resolution summary is the resolution contrast
\begin{equation}
G_i=D_{i,1}-D_{i,8}.
\label{eq:hidden-gap}
\end{equation}
A positive $G_i$ indicates that the separately normalized signed-profile discrepancy is
smaller after width-8 blocking. Because each resolution is normalized by its own
post-blocking mass, $G_i$ is an empirical contrast rather than a generally monotone
coarsening theorem and can be negative. Secondary diagnostics measure absolute response-mass transport,
onset and peak-time error, separate positive and negative mass error, and the area under
$D_{i,b}$ against $\log_2 b$. For the two-covariate mechanism, we additionally compute
$D_1$ on the joint-minus-marginal interaction response. None of these diagnostics can
replace $D_1$ or $G$ in the frozen decision.

To separate the effect of blocking from repeated normalization, we subsequently
defined a \emph{post-primary} fixed-fine-normalization diagnostic. For nonzero
responses, let $p_i=\widehat\Delta_i/\|\widehat\Delta_i\|_1$ and
$o_i=\Delta_i^\star/\|\Delta_i^\star\|_1$, and set
\begin{equation}
C_{i,b}=\tfrac12\|B_b(p_i-o_i)\|_1,\qquad H_i=C_{i,1}-C_{i,8}.
\label{eq:fixed-fine}
\end{equation}
For a numerically zero prediction, $C_{i,b}=1$ at every width, matching the
primary zero-response convention. Unlike $D_{i,b}$, $C_{i,b}$ is nonincreasing
over nested widths: $B_{2b}=B_2\circ B_b$ and the triangle inequality gives
$\|B_2u\|_1\leq\|u\|_1$. This algebraic contraction does not imply that the
registered $G_i$ is always nonnegative. The new diagnostic was applied only to
archived responses and did not change the primary endpoint or gate.

\section{CovIntervene-SHAPE Protocol}
Each scenario follows
\begin{equation}
y_t=\phi_y y_{t-1}+\beta f_m(\mathbf{x}_{1:t};\theta_m)+\eta_t,
\label{eq:generator}
\end{equation}
where covariates are autoregressive, $\eta_t$ is shared across paired worlds, and
$f_m$ defines one of four untouched mechanisms. The target autoregressive coefficient,
covariate persistence, response coefficient, and innovation scale are sampled from
frozen ranges. Intervention directions and coefficient signs are balanced across
series. A smooth pulse of length 3, 5, or 7 begins at horizon index 1, 7, or 13; its
amplitude is $0.65$ context standard deviations and active values are clipped to the
0.5th--99.5th context percentiles.

\paragraph{Biphasic rebound.}
The active covariate is convolved with an 11-step kernel containing an early positive
lobe and a delayed opposite-signed lobe. Randomized lobe centers and rebound strength
test whether a model preserves cancellation and rebound timing rather than only total
gain.

\paragraph{Dispersed delayed pulse.}
The covariate enters through a positive 12-step delay kernel with randomized onset and
dispersion. This isolates whether response onset, peak, and width follow the structural
transmission path.

\paragraph{Rate-asymmetric hysteresis.}
A latent state obeys
$s_t=\rho s_{t-1}+g(\Delta x_t)\Delta x_t$, where the gain differs for rising and
falling covariate changes; $f_m=\tanh(s_t/s_0)$. Matched paths can therefore have equal
total variation but different persistent responses.

\paragraph{Two-covariate synergy.}
The mechanism combines linear main effects with
$\gamma\tanh(x_{1,t}/s)\tanh(x_{2,t}/s)$. Factual, A-only, B-only, and joint worlds are
replayed with shared innovations. The structural interaction is
\begin{equation}
\Delta^{\star,\mathrm{int}}=\Delta^{\star,AB}-
\Delta^{\star,A}-\Delta^{\star,B},
\end{equation}
with an analogous predicted contrast. Generator assertions reject negligible
interaction responses.

The four mechanisms, their parameter ranges, three generator seeds, metric code, model
revisions, and decision thresholds were hash-locked before P1 model inference. P0
arrays and estimates were excluded from their construction. Smoke notebooks could not
compute the aggregate decision, while the analysis stage refused incomplete or
hash-mismatched units. This separation prevented run-time inspection from changing the
evaluation contract.

\section{Construct Validation}
Metric behavior was validated with analytic responses before either checkpoint was
loaded. Exact-oracle and positive-gain fixtures established identity and gain--shape
separation. Within-block and cross-block shifts tested the intended resolution
dependence; smoothing, rebound deletion, sign reversal, zero response, and interaction
omission tested distinct failure modes. Table~\ref{tab:construct-validation} reports
the selected fixture values. In particular, a one-step shift yielded $D_1=0.5$ but
$D_8=0$, whereas a seven-step shift remained visible at both resolutions. Positive
gain scaling changed RGR to 0.70 without changing shape distance.

\input{results/p1_construct_table}

All 11 blocking assertions passed. The generator preflight then reproduced 768 finite,
deterministic, nondegenerate scenarios---192 for each mechanism---with valid paired
replay. Only after these checks were complete were the numerical bounds and inference
configuration frozen. Thus, fixture results could shape the metric contract, whereas
TSFM outcomes could not.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Models and evaluation matrix}

We evaluated the official \texttt{amazon/chronos-2} and
\texttt{google/timesfm-3.0-pytorch} checkpoints at pinned revisions through their
native covariate interfaces \cite{ansari2025chronos2,jain2026timesfm3}. Both remained
frozen and zero-shot: no weights, prompts, or covariate subsets were tuned. Each model
received a 192-step context and produced a 24-step probabilistic forecast. A matched
target-only call used the same checkpoint and target history.

The primary matrix crossed two backbones with four mechanisms. Each cell contained 64
series under each of seeds 101, 307, and 911, for 192 paired series and 1,536 primary
series-level evaluations overall. We retained all eight cells regardless of outcome.
Factual-world forecast skill was summarized with scaled quantile loss (SQL) and
weighted quantile loss (WQL), using the quantiles returned by each frozen interface.
Quantile loss is a proper probabilistic scoring construction
\cite{gneiting2007proper}; scaling used context first differences to reduce dependence
on series magnitude \cite{hyndman2006accuracy}. The covariate-aware versus target-only
complement was
$(\mathrm{SQL}_{\mathrm{cov}}-\mathrm{SQL}_{\mathrm{target}})/
\mathrm{SQL}_{\mathrm{target}}$. Leakage and comparison controls followed standard
forecast-evaluation discipline \cite{hewamalage2023evaluation}.

\subsection{Uncertainty and frozen decision}

DSA and SQL were aggregated by the mean; RGR, $D_b$, and $G$ by the median. We used
5,000 deterministic percentile bootstrap replicates, resampling complete paired series
within each generator seed. A cell passed only when all four predeclared conditions
held: the DSA lower 95\% bound was at least 0.85; the complete RGR interval lay inside
$[0.65,1.35]$; the $D_1$ lower bound was at least 0.08; and the $G$ lower bound was at
least 0.03. The paper-level gate required at least two passing cells spanning two
mechanisms or both backbones, plus one passing cell whose covariate-aware SQL was no
worse than target-only by more than 2\%. All thresholds were frozen before the first
P1 checkpoint call and could not be changed afterward.

Ridge-ARX and nonlinear dynamic regression were later fitted separately using only
each scenario's 192-step context; supervised rows began at context index 24. Ridge-ARX
used target lags
$\{1,2,3,6,12,24\}$ and covariate lags 0--12 with fixed ridge penalty 1
\cite{hoerl1970ridge}. The nonlinear reference added fixed hyperbolic-tangent, square,
same-lag interaction, and signed-difference features with the same penalty. This design
was frozen only after the primary P1 outcome, used no future targets or TSFM outputs,
and is reported strictly as post-primary descriptive context.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Fine distortion and resolution contrast under coarse eligibility}

Three of eight cells passed the complete frozen rule: Chronos-2 on biphasic rebound
and dispersed delayed pulse, and TimesFM-3 on biphasic rebound
(Table~\ref{tab:p1-shape-primary}). Across these cells, DSA was 0.973--0.982 and the
complete RGR intervals remained inside the frozen $[0.65,1.35]$ envelope. Nevertheless,
$D_1$ was 0.185--0.198 and the registered resolution contrast was 0.040--0.066, with all lower
95\% bounds above the corresponding frozen thresholds. Relative SQL was
$-0.514$ to $-0.523$, so covariate-aware forecasts also improved on their matched
target-only calls in these cells.

The complete matrix also defines where the central claim does not apply. All eight
cells had a $D_1$ lower bound above 0.08, and six had a $G$ lower bound above 0.03, but

eligibility requirements, and the two hysteresis cells did not establish positive
registered resolution contrasts. TimesFM-3 on the dispersed delayed pulse missed the complete

These retained nonpassing cells prevent a generic claim that every response-shape
error is hidden behind credible coarse behavior.

Figure~\ref{fig:p2-representatives} exposes the horizon-wise behavior behind the
aggregate result without selecting an unusually vivid example: the frozen rule
chooses one median-proximal series from each passing cell. The three examples have
$D_1=0.178$, $0.192$, and $0.187$, respectively; their registered contrasts are
$0.044$, $0.068$, and $0.040$. They are not average responses.

The archived response arrays support the fixed-fine-normalization check in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fixed-fine}. All individual curves, and hence all eight displayed
cell medians, were nonincreasing over widths 1, 2, 4, and 8
(Fig.~\ref{fig:p2-resolution}). The median \emph{paired} contraction $H_i$ ranged
from $0.041$ to $0.120$ across cells; its eight bootstrap lower bounds ranged
from $0.034$ to $0.102$. These values are not obtained by subtracting the two
reported marginal medians. In the three registered passing cells, the paired
median contractions were $0.069$, $0.054$, and $0.053$. This check confirms that
fine response discrepancies are reduced by signed blocking under a metric with a
mathematical contraction guarantee, without retroactively reinterpreting $G$.

The same analytic fixture ladder permits a descriptive comparison with common
trajectory diagnostics (Table~\ref{tab:p2-metric-comparison}). Positive gain
scaling yields RGR $0.70$ and NRMSE $0.30$ but $D_1=0$. A within-width-8 shift
has $D_1=0.50$ and $G=0.50$, whereas normalized dynamic time warping (DTW)
assigns zero to both one- and seven-step shifts in these fixtures. Conversely,
deleting the rebound yields $D_1=0.38$ but $G=0$: a coarse-versus-fine contrast
alone does not detect every structural omission. Absolute-mass transport assigns
zero to the sign-flip fixture even though $D_1=1$. These are differences in
metric invariance on constructed examples, not evidence of universal dominance.

\subsection{Post-primary transparent references}

\input{results/p1_reference_table}
The post-primary references provide a mechanism-level check rather than another
continuation test. Ridge-ARX nearly recovered the two additive temporal responses
($D_1=0.038$ and $0.057$), but its hysteresis response remained distorted
($D_1=0.243$) and its synergy interaction distance was 1.000. The frozen nonlinear
feature expansion reduced the synergy interaction distance to 0.300 and changed its
DSA/RGR from 0.784/0.724 to 0.871/1.042. It was not uniformly stronger: its $D_1$ and
SQL were both higher than Ridge-ARX on the two additive mechanisms. These fitted
references therefore show that low response-shape error is attainable under the same
context length while also exposing mechanism-specific limitations; they do not enter
or modify the primary decision.

\subsection{Post-primary semi-synthetic source transfer}
The extension used real target-series backgrounds but synthetic covariates and
structural responses; it was not an observational causal test. Its first,
construct-only P3-v1 design failed the frozen 5\% exclusion ceiling in three of
six source--mechanism cells: 38 of 864 attempted scenarios had insufficient
oracle mass. No checkpoint was evaluated under that design. A disclosed P3-v2
redesign increased the covariate pulse and moved dispersed-pulse starts using
only this construct diagnostic, before P3-v2 model inference. The unchanged
oracle-mass criterion then excluded five of 576 scenarios. Each frozen
backbone was evaluated on the remaining 571 scenarios, drawn from 32
previously unused original IDs per source and three new seeds. Intervals
resampled original IDs with all their valid seeds together.

\input{results/p3_v2_transfer_table}
Three of the twelve evaluated cells passed the unchanged P1 complete-cell rule
descriptively: both Chronos-2 traffic mechanisms and the TimesFM-3 traffic
biphasic mechanism (Table~\ref{tab:p3-v2-transfer}). Their covariate-aware SQL
was 45.9--50.9\% lower than the matched target-only SQL, while lower bounds on
$D_1$ and $G$ remained positive and above the P1 thresholds. Yet no workload
or solar cell passed. All twelve cells cleared the $D_1$ lower-bound threshold;
outside traffic, the coarse direction and gain requirements generally failed.
Because the predeclared transfer rule required a complete cell in at least two
of the three sources, this extension \emph{did not} establish source transfer.
It neither changes the P1 primary decision nor converts the traffic-only
pattern into a generalization claim.

Across cells, median sham responses amounted to 0.055--0.535 of active-oracle
mass. This nonzero placebo response precludes a perfect-specificity claim.
Lower-link medians and oracle paths support descriptive response
shape summaries, but lower-link quantiles were not archived. Lower-link relative
SQL is therefore unavailable rather than zero or imputed; no lower-link
forecast-skill sensitivity claim is made.

\section{Analysis and Limitations}
The primary result separates predictive usefulness from structural response
faithfulness. In the three passing cells, supplying covariates substantially reduced
SQL and produced credible direction and total gain, yet the fine-resolution forecast
response still differed from the oracle shape. This combination matters because forecast
accuracy alone can reward a model for using the target history and covariates in any
predictively useful way. It does not require the local response to a specific future
path to match the response encoded by the data-generating mechanism.

The finding complements, rather than contradicts, accuracy-centered covariate
forecasting studies. ChronosX, COSMIC, UniCA, TFMAdapter, CITRAS-FM, and ApolloPFN ask
how exogenous information can improve predictions or adaptation
\cite{arango2025chronosx,auer2025cosmic,han2025unica,dange2025tfmadapter,
yamaguchi2026citrasfm,potapczynski2026apollopfn}. Our evidence indicates that an
additional response-level audit can reveal information not contained in an aggregate
forecast score. It also narrows the connection to prior intervention studies: the
tested object here is the external known-future path and the measured object is the
signed target-response trajectory, rather than a generator parameter, internal
representation, or optimized counterfactual.

The fitted references help interpret attainability without defining a new primary
comparison. Ridge-ARX's low shape distances on the two additive temporal mechanisms
show that the metric does not impose uniformly unreachable fidelity under a 192-step
context. Its failure on the registered interaction and the nonlinear reference's
partial recovery indicate that mechanism structure matters. Because these references
were specified after the primary outcome and fitted to each scenario, they cannot
support a zero-shot superiority ranking against either TSFM.

Five boundaries constrain the conclusion. First, analytic oracle responses are
available because the mechanisms are synthetic. This provides internal structural
validity but does not establish causal faithfulness on observational applications.
Second, two public checkpoints support a cross-architecture result only for their
pinned revisions; they do not establish a property of all TSFMs. Third, the 24-step
horizon and widths $\{1,2,4,8\}$ operationalize one temporal scale. Other horizons or
task-specific resolutions may change how strongly discrepancy attenuates. Fourth, the numerical
bounds were adjusted once using only analytic fixtures, before model outputs existed,
and were then frozen. The resulting test is auditable, but its thresholds should be
treated as an explicit operating definition rather than a universal notion of
practical significance.
Fifth, the post-primary semi-synthetic audit did not meet its two-source
transfer rule. Its traffic-only complete cells cannot establish response
faithfulness across real-series backgrounds, especially with possible
pretraining overlap, context-based activity selection, and only 32 original
ID clusters per source. Source and checkpoint license terms may constrain
redistribution of the private archives.

A useful next test would pair real interventions or controlled policy changes with a
known-future covariate and an independently defensible response trajectory. Such data
would test transport beyond synthetic mechanisms without pretending that ordinary
observational correlations supply an oracle. A broader checkpoint study should
likewise freeze interfaces, revisions, and decision rules before accessing new model
outcomes.

\section{Conclusion}
CovIntervene-SHAPE audits how a frozen forecaster's response to a known-future
covariate is distributed across the forecast horizon. Analytic fixtures established
that the signed multi-resolution distance distinguishes gain error, temporal
displacement, smoothing, rebound deletion, and interaction omission. In a frozen
two-backbone, four-mechanism evaluation, three cells combined credible coarse response
and improved forecast skill with fine shape distortion and a positive registered
resolution contrast. The result shows that, for the tested checkpoints and mechanisms,
direction, gain, and forecast accuracy do not fully characterize covariate-response
faithfulness. Extending this conclusion to operational forecasting requires response
oracles or controlled interventions beyond the synthetic setting. The later
semi-synthetic audit did not reproduce the complete pattern outside traffic;
transfer across sources remains unestablished.
